What Europe's carbon rules mean for India

Three EU rules now touch Indian industry and Indian carbon credits: the border carbon charge on exports (CBAM), the EU carbon price, and the EU's plan to accept Paris Agreement credits from 2036. This page puts them next to India's own market.

Where CBAM and India's CCTS meet

An Indian plant in an overlapping sector faces both: a CCTS emission-intensity target at home and a CBAM charge on what it sells to Europe. The CBAM rules provide for the carbon price already paid at home to be deducted.

The EU carbon removal standard (CRCF)

Since 7 May 2026 the EU certifies permanent carbon removals (biochar, direct air capture with storage, bio-energy with carbon capture and storage) under one public method. Its location rules point to EU-based projects, so Indian projects cannot use it directly today, but it is the quality yardstick European buyers will compare Indian biochar and removal credits against.
