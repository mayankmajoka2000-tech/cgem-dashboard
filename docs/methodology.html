How the C-GEM price model works

A transparent, tested estimate of what an Indian voluntary carbon credit is worth, with an honest range. Every price the model learns from is listed below with its source, and every test result is published, including the ones where it got worse.

How it differs from paid price benchmarks

Paid benchmark services such as Platts, Fastmarkets or MSCI assess prices from daily conversations with traders and from private deal data. That is something a public-data model cannot match, and their assessments are part of this model’s training data where they were published. This model is built for different jobs:

NeedThis model
A price for a specific Indian project5,013 projects priced individually by type, registry, vintage and CCP label

Checking how a price was madeEquation, every coefficient, every training price and every test are published
Turning a price into a decisionProject finance, risk simulation, buyer portfolio, forward curve, CCTS and CBAM tools on the same model
Daily traded market levelsNot offered. Use a paid benchmark for daily marks, and this model to check and extend them

How the model works

  1. Collect public prices. Market averages, benchmark assessments, named trades, offers and marketplace listings from 2020 to 2026, each with its date, credit type, region, standard, vintage, rating, labels and source link.
  2. Describe each price by its features. Year, credit type, region and India, standard, removal or reduction, recent or old vintage, high or low rating, Article 6 / CORSIA authorisation, CCP label, and whether it is an offer, a forward or a listing.
  3. Fit a robust hedonic model on log price. 0.8 × Huber regression, which keeps outlier deals from dominating, plus 0.2 × a hierarchical ridge pulled toward premiums published in the literature. Each older year counts 0.7× the next.
  4. Let effects vary where the data shows they do. Separate slopes for vintage and rating by credit type, Asia and India effects by type, a separate Article 6 / CORSIA effect for 2026, when CORSIA Phase 1 prices fell from about $23 to about $10 (from version 17), and a separate CORSIA premium for cookstoves (from version 18).
  5. Give every price a range. P10 and P90 come from split-conformal prediction on leave-one-out errors, wider for credit types with fewer than six wholesale prices.
  6. Test before release. A new version ships only if it beats the last one on Indian deal prices it did not see.

How it is tested

What the tests mean

  • Leave-one-out: fit on every price but one, predict that one, repeat for each.
  • Leave-one-publisher-out: drop every price from one publisher, predict them. This checks the model does not just copy one source.
  • Time split: fit on prices up to 2024, predict 2025–26 as if they were 2024. This is the hardest test and the weakest result, because 2025–26 prices moved for reasons earlier data cannot show.
  • Honest error on new research prices: how the previous version did on prices added after it was built.

Check against Climate Impact X (June 2026)

Climate Impact X published Indian agri-carbon prices from its own trades, bids and offers (339 data points, 2022–2026), commissioned by the Gates Foundation. v18 was built before C-GEM had these prices, so the v18 column is an independent test: both Indian agri-carbon prices fell inside the CIX range. v20 then adds the two CIX averages as training prices.

Governance and limits

  • Public data only. Private deals and paywalled assessment series are not included unless their figures were published. Several Indian credit types still rest on fewer than five wholesale prices; their confidence is marked Low.
  • No public price yet for Indian rice methane deals beyond one 2025 offtake report, N₂O or HFC-23 deals, livestock methane deals, Indian mangrove credits, or CCTS carbon credit certificates.
  • Project prices. Each project in the dashboard gets its credit type’s price, adjusted for its registry (Verra ×0.71, Gold Standard ×1.32, CDM ×0.29, Plan Vivo ×1.71, Cercarbono ×0.63; others ×1.0), an old vintage and a CCP label where known. On the 9 Indian deal prices with a known standard, the registry adjustment cut median error from 40% to 26%; the sample is small. Use the calculator to add a project’s rating or Article 6 status.
  • Change control. Every candidate change is tested on the same held-out prices, and the result is recorded in the version history whether it ships or not.
  • Not investment advice. Estimates are for screening, budgeting and negotiation support. Check any price against a current quote before you buy or sell.
